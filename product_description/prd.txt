# Investment Intelligence Platform — Overview PRD

**Status:** Product Requirements Document  
**Audience:** Product, engineering, quantitative research, ML/agent engineering, design, and founding team  
**Purpose:** High-level product specification for an evidence-backed, Process-first investment intelligence SaaS.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The Investment Intelligence Platform is a research and decision-support system designed to identify, model, monitor, and evaluate emerging economic Processes and translate them into investable Asset expressions.

The central premise is that large investment opportunities often arise not from isolated securities but from **underlying economic Processes** whose consequences propagate through Bottlenecks, Capabilities, and ultimately Assets.

The system therefore models:

> **Evidence → Events → Economic Processes → Process States → Bottlenecks → Capabilities → Assets → Trades**

The product is intended to help users discover emerging trends, understand how those trends are evolving, identify the constraints and capabilities they create, find the Assets best positioned to benefit, construct investment cases, monitor those theses over time, and evaluate the system's own historical performance.

The product should not function primarily as an LLM chatbot that gives stock recommendations. LLMs are one component of a larger architecture involving structured data, event extraction, graph relationships, quantitative analysis, historical analogues, evidence provenance, iterative critique, and explicit evaluation.

This document is intentionally overview-level. Detailed specifications are maintained in the companion documents:

- `economic_process_asset_ontology.md` — canonical ontology and domain model.
- `agent_architecture_and_evaluation.md` — agents, responsibilities, prompts, orchestration, and evaluation.
- `ui_concept.md` — detailed UX/UI architecture and user interaction model.

# 2. Product Vision

Build a continuously updated representation of the economic state space that allows an investor to move from:

> **"What is changing?"**

to:

> **"What is the strongest investable expression of that change?"**

while retaining a complete chain of reasoning and evidence.

The product should make it possible to inspect not only the system's conclusion, but:

- what it believes,
- why it believes it,
- what evidence changed that belief,
- which assumptions are critical,
- which historical episodes are comparable,
- what could invalidate the thesis,
- and how the resulting Asset expression is performing.

The long-term vision is a research system that combines the strengths of:

- LLM reasoning,
- structured economic modeling,
- quantitative analysis,
- historical pattern matching,
- graph-based inference,
- and human investment judgment.

---

# 3. Problem Statement

Traditional investment research tools are generally organized around securities, documents, prices, or broad market categories.

This creates several problems when researching large emerging trends:

1. A major economic development may affect many industries and Asset classes.
2. The most obvious Asset may not be the best expression of the underlying Process.
3. Multiple upstream Processes may converge on a single Capability.
4. A Process evolves through identifiable stages, and the same Asset can have very different opportunity characteristics at different stages.
5. News is fragmented across thousands of documents.
6. Evidence is duplicated and correlated.
7. LLM-generated investment advice can sound authoritative without providing a robust causal or quantitative foundation.
8. Historical analogues are difficult to identify and compare systematically.
9. Existing tools generally do not preserve a persistent, auditable history of why an investment thesis changed.

The platform addresses these problems by treating the **Economic Process and its evolution** as the primary unit of research.

# 4. Product Principles

## 4.1 Process-first

The system should begin with economic Processes rather than securities.

An Asset is an investable projection of an upstream Process/Capability configuration.

---

## 4.2 Evidence-backed

Important conclusions must be traceable to source evidence.

Every material statement should be attributable to:

- a document,
- an Event,
- a quantitative observation,
- a historical observation,
- or an explicitly identified model inference.

---

## 4.3 Temporal

Processes are dynamic.

The system must preserve:

- historical State,
- State transitions,
- evidence accumulation,
- score changes,
- and point-in-time information availability.

---

## 4.4 Explicit uncertainty

The system should distinguish between:

- evidence,
- inference,
- model belief,
- historical analogy,
- and speculation.

It should not present uncertain model outputs as facts.

---

## 4.5 Thesis quality ≠ Asset quality

The system should separately evaluate:

### Thesis quality

How compelling is the underlying Process?

### Asset expression quality

How attractive is a particular Asset as an expression of that Process?

This distinction is fundamental to the product.

---

## 4.6 LLMs are components, not authorities

LLMs should be used for tasks such as:

- extraction,
- semantic classification,
- hypothesis generation,
- causal reasoning,
- synthesis,
- critique,
- and natural-language interaction.

They should be constrained by:

- structured ontology,
- source grounding,
- deterministic calculations,
- quantitative tools,
- historical data,
- independent critique,
- and evaluation.

# 6. Core User Jobs

The product should support five principal jobs.

## 6.1 Find hot trends

Identify Processes where:

- evidence is accelerating,
- State is changing,
- Bottlenecks are emerging,
- or market attention may be lagging.

---

## 6.2 Track Process evolution

Understand:

- current Process State,
- State trajectory,
- evidence accumulation,
- contradictions,
- Bottleneck evolution,
- and historical analogues.

---

## 6.3 Research investment ideas

Move from:

> Process → Bottleneck → Capability → Asset

and identify multiple possible expressions.

---

## 6.4 Build an investment case

For a particular Asset, determine:

- Process exposure,
- Capability exposure,
- idiosyncratic advantages,
- financial characteristics,
- valuation,
- technical behavior,
- crowding,
- historical analogues,
- counterarguments,
- and thesis risks.

---

## 6.5 Monitor trades

Monitor whether:

- the Process is strengthening,
- the Bottleneck persists,
- Capability requirements remain valid,
- Asset exposure remains intact,
- and Asset-specific conditions remain favorable.

The system should monitor **thesis integrity**, not merely price.

# 7. Core Domain Model

The detailed ontology is specified in:

> `economic_process_asset_ontology.md`

At overview level, the system contains the following principal objects.

### Evidence / Document

The raw information entering the system.

### Event

A normalized occurrence extracted from one or more documents.

### Economic Process

A persistent economic phenomenon evolving through time.

### Process State

The current stage of a Process.

Examples may include:

- Initial Discovery
- Early Adoption
- Acceleration
- Infrastructure Expansion
- Euphoria
- Saturation
- Maturity

The exact State machine is archetype-specific.

### Bottleneck

A constraint that prevents or limits the Process from achieving its implied outcome.

### Capability

A concrete capability required to satisfy a Bottleneck or participate in a Process.

### Asset

An investable security or instrument providing exposure to one or more Capabilities and Processes.

### Trade

A future implementation layer for expressing an Asset thesis, initially limited in scope.

# 8. Causal / Translation Model

The primary research flow is:

```text
Documents
    ↓
Events
    ↓
Economic Processes
    ↓
Process State
    ↓
Bottlenecks
    ↓
Capabilities
    ↓
Assets
    ↓
Trade

The graph is not strictly linear.

Multiple upstream Processes can feed a single downstream Process, Bottleneck, or Capability.

Example:

AI Infrastructure ─────┐
                       ├──► Domestic Semiconductor Fabs
CHIPS Act ─────────────┘

Likewise, a single Process may create multiple downstream branches:

AI Adoption
    ├──► Chips
    ├──► Memory
    ├──► Networking
    ├──► Power
    ├──► Cooling
    └──► Cloud Compute

This allows the system to identify confluences rather than treating every Process as an isolated thematic bucket.

# 9. Process Archetypes

The system should organize Processes into archetypes because different Processes:

evolve differently,
implicate different Asset classes,
have different State machines,
have different measurable indicators,
and have different historical analogues.

Initial archetypes include:

Infrastructure / S-curve adoption
Commodity supply cycles
Industrial bottlenecks
Regulatory implementation
Business model disruption

Additional archetypes can be introduced as historical evaluation demonstrates their usefulness.

Archetype-specific behavior should govern:

State definitions,
indicators,
historical matching,
likely Asset classes,
and evaluation metrics.

# 10. Investment Thesis Architecture

A thesis should not simply be a textual statement.

It should be a structured object containing:

upstream Processes,
current State,
Bottlenecks,
Capabilities,
supporting evidence,
contradictory evidence,
historical precedent,
counterfactuals,
confidence dimensions,
candidate Assets,
and temporal evolution.

Thesis quality should be multidimensional.

Potential dimensions include:

Logical Coherence
Accumulated Evidence
Evidence Independence
Process-State Confidence
Historical Precedent
Counterfactual Robustness
Magnitude / Economic Scope
Timing / Stage Quality

The system should avoid reducing these dimensions to a single unexplained percentage.

# 11. Asset Expression Architecture

Asset selection is a separate layer.

For each candidate Asset, evaluate dimensions such as:

Process exposure,
Capability exposure,
financial quality,
first-mover advantage,
vertical integration,
market share,
strategic positioning,
institutional ownership,
valuation,
technical behavior,
liquidity,
crowding,
and other idiosyncratic characteristics.

The system should explicitly answer:

Is this a good Process?

and separately:

Is this a good Asset expression of that Process?

# 12. Historical Learning

Historical analogues are central to the long-term system.

The system should not merely find similar companies or headlines.

It should identify:

comparable Process archetypes,
comparable Process States,
comparable Bottlenecks,
comparable Capability configurations,
Assets with similar characteristics,
and subsequent historical outcomes.

The goal is eventually to estimate how the current Process configuration has historically evolved.

This provides an empirical complement to LLM reasoning.

The initial system should focus on historical pattern matching and conditional comparison, not automatically generating highly precise future probability distributions.

# 14. Evidence Processing

A major design requirement is evidence deduplication.

Multiple documents may describe the same underlying Event.

The pipeline should therefore distinguish:

Document
    ↓
Document extraction
    ↓
Event candidate
    ↓
Event deduplication / clustering
    ↓
Canonical Event
    ↓
Process update

This prevents a single news event from artificially generating dozens of independent pieces of evidence.

Evidence should retain provenance to every contributing document.

---

# 15. Update Architecture

Updates should occur in stages rather than propagating every document through the entire graph synchronously.

Recommended conceptual flow:

Documents
    ↓
Extraction
    ↓
Event accumulation / deduplication
    ↓
Event-triggered Process update
    ↓
Process-level evidence accumulation
    ↓
Capability / Asset update
    ↓
Ranking / alert generation

Updates may be triggered by:

time thresholds,
evidence-count thresholds,
materiality thresholds,
State-change candidates,
or explicit user requests.

This limits unnecessary LLM calls and creates a manageable audit trail.

# 18. Natural-Language Interface

The platform should provide an LLM-driven research interface, but it should be grounded in structured platform data.

Examples:

"Why did this Process score increase this week?"

"Which Capabilities are downstream of this Process?"

"Which Assets have exposure to both Processes?"

"What evidence contradicts this thesis?"

"Find historical episodes where this Process archetype was in the same State."

"Why is Asset A ranked above Asset B?"

The assistant should retrieve and reason over structured context rather than generate unsupported conclusions.

# 21. Research Journal and Temporal Audit

Every Process and monitored Asset should maintain a chronological journal.

Example:

2026-06-12
State confidence 7.2 → 7.8

+ Utility CapEx
+ Interconnection acceleration
+ Nuclear PPAs

2026-06-27
State confidence 7.8 → 7.5

− Capacity additions
− Construction slowdown

This provides:

user transparency,
model accountability,
historical backtesting,
and training data for future system improvement.

The system must preserve point-in-time state to prevent hindsight leakage.

It should support:

financial comparison,
valuation,
operating metrics,
price behavior,
volume,
ownership,
volatility,
technical structure,
and relative ranking.

The initial product should not attempt full Trade Quant.

In particular, options positioning and highly detailed conditional probability distributions can remain a later phase.

However, the architecture should preserve the ability to add:

Process State → conditional Asset outcome distribution → horizon-specific trade construction

in a future version.

# 28. Success Criteria

The MVP is successful if it demonstrates that the platform can reliably perform the following loop:

Detect a meaningful Process.
Aggregate and deduplicate relevant Events.
Determine a defensible Process State.
Identify important Bottlenecks.
Translate Bottlenecks into Capabilities.
Identify multiple Asset expressions.
Quantitatively compare Assets.
Preserve evidence provenance.
Construct an inspectable investment case.
Monitor changes to the thesis over time.
Learn from historical outcomes.

The system does not need to demonstrate universal market prediction.

The initial objective is to establish that this structured workflow produces better research decisions than naive LLM usage or conventional thematic screening.
